## Analysis and limitations

- **Grip utilization.** Calibrating on the drivers' reconstructed lines over-estimates grip: at ~3.7 Hz the positions are up to 20 m apart, the reconstructed curvature is noisy, and the fit raises grip to recover the real lap time. Noise-free optimized lines then come out ~10 % faster than pole. A grip-utilization factor γ (scaling the tyre friction coefficients) is therefore fitted per official circuit type (OpenF1: street vs permanent/road) so that model optima match real poles on average; it also absorbs what a point mass cannot do (yaw/roll transients, walls, bumps). The held-out column is the honest prediction error.
- **Practice correlation.** As teams correlate their simulator to Friday/Saturday running, the grip level of each weekend is tuned so the optimal lap equals the best pre-qualifying lap times the typical practice-to-pole ratio of the *other* races with the same session type (FP3 or sprint qualifying). The physics then decides the line, speeds and where time is won; the lap-time accuracy mostly comes from the practice pace.
- **Weekend setup.** Downforce and drag are refitted per weekend on the last session before qualifying (practice or sprint qualifying), so wing level follows the circuit. Setup alone did not help (held-out mean |gap| 4.5 %), grip by circuit type alone gave 3.0 %, both together 3.2 % mean and 2.8 % median with the worst case cut from 11.5 % to 8.6 %.
- **Replay error is large by construction** once γ < 1: the noisy reconstructed driver line is slower in the model than it is in reality.
- **Min-curvature vs min-time.** The min-curvature line is several percent slower than the min-time line on most circuits: it spreads curvature evenly, while time is lost mostly in fast corners where the γ-limited grip binds.
- **Outliers.** Melbourne (+8.6 %) and Miami (+3.8 %) are officially street circuits but are fast park/stadium layouts, so the street grip level (fitted mostly on Monaco, Baku, Singapore, Madring) is too low for them. Madring (−8 %) and Monaco (−6 %) have short, approximate geometry (drivers' mean line / OSM ~3 % under the official length). Monza (−6 %) is the lowest-downforce circuit, where the model's slipstream-free, energy-unlimited straights flatter it.
- **2026 cars.** Active aero (straight-line mode) and the 50/50 power split with energy clipping on straights are not modelled; drag and power are effective values.
- **Not modelled:** elevation, banking, kerbs beyond the car-centre bound, tyre state, wind, traffic, DRS/overtake mode.
- **Scope.** Only the 18 rounds of 2026 that have been run are included (`season.completed_only`); remaining rounds are added by re-running the pipeline after each qualifying session.
- **Geometry.** TUM layouts are used only if the driven laps fit them (≥95 % of samples inside bounds + 1 m position tolerance); otherwise OpenStreetMap (nominal widths, widened to observed car positions). Madring has incomplete OSM data and uses the drivers' mean line (approximate).
